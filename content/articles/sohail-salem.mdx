HIS ART FOUND FREEDOM. HE IS STILL WAITING FOR HIS.

## SOHAIL SALEM | THE ART OF MEMORY AND HUMANITY

From his childhood in Gaza to artistic experiences across the world, Palestinian artist Sohail Salem has spent his life exploring humanity through drawing. Today, his artworks travel beyond the borders he cannot cross, carrying memories, emotions and stories of the place he calls home.

An exclusive interview with The Human Thread_AE

> How was I able to free some of my drawings, yet unable to free myself and my family?

This was the question that came to Palestinian artist Sohail Salem when a collection of his drawings was successfully brought out of Gaza.

Today, his artworks travel across borders, reaching audiences around the world, while he and his family remain in Gaza.

Yet behind these drawings is a lifetime of curiosity, artistic discovery and a profound connection to people, places and memory.

![](/articles/sohail-salem/01.jpg)

## A LIFE SHAPED BY ART

Sohail Salem's relationship with drawing began during his childhood in Gaza.

Without an art teacher at school, he learned through observation, experimentation and passion. He drew characters from films and everyday scenes before the events surrounding him began to influence his artistic expression.

> Through drawing, I felt that I had a free space to express myself and create my own world.

He later studied fine arts at Al-Aqsa University, following his first formal artistic training at the Young Men's Christian Association in Gaza.

His artistic journey also took him to Amman, Geneva and Paris, where encounters with different artists and cultural experiences expanded his understanding of creativity.

Among the most influential was his meeting with Syrian artist Marwan Kassab-Bachi at the September Summer Academy in Amman.

In 2002, he also helped establish Eltiqa for Contemporary Art in Gaza, creating a space for artistic dialogue, collaboration and shared creativity.

These experiences helped shape his belief that art reaches far beyond the canvas.

> Art is not only what we draw, but the way we look at things, the questions we carry, and our relationship with people, place and memory.

## THE HUMAN BEING BEHIND EVERY LINE

Faces and human figures are central to Sohail Salem's work.

Yet he is interested in more than their appearance.

> When I draw a face or a body, I am not only searching for external features. I try to approach what is hidden behind them.

For him, every face carries memories, emotions and experiences.

His drawings explore vulnerability, strength and the relationship between people and the places that shape their lives.

But he is careful not to define anyone through suffering alone.

> I do not want to reduce the human being to suffering, or make pain their only identity.

This belief is at the heart of his work: every person carries a story larger than a single moment.

## THE GAZA HE WANTS US TO REMEMBER

When Sohail Salem speaks about Gaza, his memories are filled with the beauty of everyday life.

The sea, childhood friendships, the streets of the old town, conversations between neighbours and the familiar smell of cooking.

He remembers the morning breeze and the blue horizon that once brought him a sense of calm and freedom.

> What matters to me about these details is that they are not passing or marginal things. They are life itself.

These memories find their way into his drawings through colours, faces and familiar places.

> Gaza is a society with its own culture, memory, beauty and daily life. It is not a story of war.

For Sohail Salem, preserving these details is as important as expressing the difficulties surrounding him.

He wants people to remember that life is also made of dreams, creativity, relationships and moments of beauty.

## ART BEYOND CIRCUMSTANCES

Displacement profoundly changed Sohail Salem's relationship with drawing.

Without access to his usual studio and materials, he began working with whatever was available.

Ink pens, school notebooks and sometimes even electrocardiogram paper became part of his creative practice.

These simple materials encouraged him to focus on the emotion behind every line.

Yet he does not romanticise the circumstances that made them necessary.

> This does not mean that I chose these circumstances, or that I see deprivation as an artistic value in itself.

During a particularly difficult experience, he was detained and separated from his family. He recalls the helplessness of not knowing what would happen to them.

He drew himself during his detention, transforming an experience that was difficult to express in words into a visual memory.

> Behind every line there is an experience, and behind every drawing there is a life that cannot be reduced to a single scene.

For Sohail Salem, art remains a way of understanding life and creating human connections beyond geographical boundaries.

## THE FREEDOM TO KEEP DREAMING

Despite the uncertainty surrounding his future, Sohail Salem continues to dream of creating.

He hopes to develop a larger artistic project, revisit earlier works and find the freedom to experiment and discover new possibilities.

> Perhaps the most beautiful thing about a dream is that it remains open. It gives me a reason to continue and invites me to discover what we have not yet known about ourselves and the world.

When asked what he hopes will remain of him through his work, his answer is not about recognition or artistic success.

It is about the impact his drawings may have on others.

> I do not think much about whether my works will remain as artistic achievements. I think more about the impact they may leave on the person who sees them.

He hopes that someone who has never met him or visited Gaza might one day look at his drawings and recognise something human within them.

His final wish reflects the purpose behind his lifelong relationship with art.

> I do not wish for people to remember my name alone, but to find in my works a reason to reflect on the human being, on the right to life, freedom and dignity, and on our ability to see one another beyond images and preconceived judgments.

From a curious child discovering drawing to an artist whose work now reaches audiences around the world, Sohail Salem has continued to explore what it means to be human.

His artworks have travelled beyond Gaza. Through them, his memories, creativity and humanity continue to reach the world.
